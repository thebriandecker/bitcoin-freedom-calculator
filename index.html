The old retirement plan makes you sell your best asset. This one tests keeping it.

The usual retirement question is "how big does my pile need to be before I can start selling pieces of it?" Every year you sell, your pile shrinks, and your best-performing asset stops working for you.

This calculator tests a different structure: keep the whole Bitcoin stack, and borrow against it each year to create your income. The stack keeps growing. Nothing gets sold. But — and this is the part most people skip — every loan is a real debt that stacks up year after year. So the honest question isn't "does one loan work?" It's:

Does the stack grow faster than the debt pile — even when Bitcoin crashes 50% along the way?

That's the only question this tool answers.

What happens each year, in plain English

Once your Freedom Year starts, the model assumes you take out one new loan every year. That new loan does 3 jobs:

  • Pays off last year's loan completely (a refinance).
  • Sets aside 12 months of interest up front, so the loan services itself.
  • Hands you your yearly income in cash.

Small worked example: you want $100K of income and the rate is 8%. The model borrows about $108.7K — $100K goes to you, $8.7K sits in reserve to cover the year's interest. Next year's loan pays that whole balance off and the cycle repeats, bigger each time.

That's why the debt line on the chart only goes up. The model never pays the debt down — it tests whether the stack can outgrow it anyway. It also quietly assumes a lender will always be there to refinance you at the same rate, with no fees. Real life is messier than that.

The safety line (LTV) is the whole game

LTV = what you owe ÷ what your stack is worth. Owe $200K against a $1M stack and you're at 20% LTV.

Here's why it's dangerous to ignore: in a crash, your debt doesn't fall with the price. If Bitcoin drops 50%, a comfortable 20% LTV instantly becomes 40% — without you borrowing another dollar.

The stack halves. The debt doesn't. Your borrowing level doubles overnight.

So you set a safety line — the highest LTV you'll tolerate anywhere in the plan, crash years included. The calculator only calls a year your Freedom Year if the full plan stays under that line all the way to 2040. Your safety line is a planning rule you choose. It is not the level where a lender would liquidate you, and it is not a recommendation.

The built-in crash test

A smooth growth forecast alone would make any borrowing plan look brilliant. So after applying the forecast you picked, the model forces the price through a 4-year boom-and-crash cycle:

PhaseWhat the model does
ClimbPrice follows the forecast's trend line
BoomPrice overshoots the trend by 60% — the blow-off top
CrashPrice falls 50% from the top (then 45%, then 40% in later cycles)
RecoverPrice returns to the trend line

The crash years are where plans die — that's exactly when your LTV spikes. The calendar placement and depths are modeling assumptions, not predictions of when real crashes will happen. You can make the test milder or harsher in the crash-test panel.

Why "wait until" is the question we model

We deliberately model one question: what's the earliest year your full target income fits inside your safety line? If the answer is 2028, that means starting in 2027 would have pushed you over the line in some crash year. Waiting longer than your Freedom Year generally just adds cushion. One more honesty rule: a start year only counts as fitting if at least one modeled crash still gets to test it — a plan that "fits" in 2040 with no crash left to face hasn't really been tested, so we don't call it a pass. If the answer is "never by 2040," the honest fixes are a bigger stack, a smaller income target, more years of stacking, or a more conservative read on what that tells you — not a bigger safety line.

WHAT THIS TOOL CANNOT TELL YOU

This models the Bitcoin math only. It does not build the system around the Bitcoin, and it knows nothing about:

  • Which lender to use — custody, rehypothecation, margin-call rules, liquidation triggers, and contract terms vary wildly and can break a plan the math says works.
  • Refinancing risk — the model assumes credit is always available at your rate. In a deep crash, it may not be.
  • Intra-year lows — the model checks your borrowing level once per year, at year-end prices. Real crashes bottom mid-year, far below year-end (2018: −84%, 2022: −77% peak-to-trough), and margin calls happen the day a line is crossed — not at year-end.
  • Taxes — a genuine loan is generally not a taxable sale, but forced liquidations, canceled debt, and moving collateral can create tax events. Talk to a professional.
  • Your full picture — income, cash reserves, other assets, family obligations, and your ability to act calmly in a 50% drawdown.

Borrowing against Bitcoin can end in liquidation and loss of your collateral. Educational scenario planning only — not financial, investment, tax, legal, or lending advice.

Nobody knows Bitcoin's future price — including these models.

Each forecast below is a published, sourced way of guessing. They use different methods and different assumptions, which is exactly why we show you all of them. Your Freedom Year moves a lot depending on which one you pick — that sensitivity is the lesson.

Don't pick the model with the biggest number. Pick the one whose story you'd still believe in a −50% year.

POWER LAW

Giovanni Santostasi (physicist) · 2024 write-up

A math-only model: Bitcoin's price has tracked a smooth curve based purely on time since it launched in Jan 2009 (price ≈ time^5.8). It fits 15+ years of history remarkably well, and its growth naturally slows every year.

METHODtime^5.8 HISTORICAL FITR² ≈ 0.95 USED HEREthrough 2040

What has to be true: the historical pattern keeps holding. Critics call it curve-fitting, and a pattern that fit the past is not a law of nature, so we only run it through 2040. No scenario picker — the curve is the curve.

Source: The Bitcoin Power Law Theory
